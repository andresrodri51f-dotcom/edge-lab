EdgeLab

Live scores and 100,000-simulation predictions for every game
No model beats the sportsbooks every time. The books' closing lines are very accurate, so real edges are small: usually 1 to 4%. This tool shows where your numbers disagree with the line. It's only as good as the stats you enter. Bet small amounts you can afford to lose.

What the model does

NBA: It estimates possessions from both teams' pace, then points per possession from each offense's rating against the other team's defensive rating. It blends season and last-10 numbers, then adds home court, back-to-backs and injuries. Each simulated game varies the pace, a shared "shooting night" effect that moves both scores together, and each team's own performance. Tied games go to overtime.

NFL: It simulates every drive (about 11 per team). Each drive ends in a touchdown (sometimes 6 or 8 points), a field goal, or nothing, with chances scaled to each team's expected scoring. That reproduces football's key margins of 3 and 7, which simpler models miss.

MLB: Expected runs come from each offense's runs per game and the opposing pitching staff. That's a weighted mix of the starter's ERA/FIP for the innings he's expected to pitch and the bullpen for the rest, adjusted for the park and home field. Runs follow an overdispersed (negative binomial) distribution, which matches baseball's blowouts and shutouts. Extra innings break ties.

Props: The projection blends the season average with recent form, then adjusts for role/minutes, opponent and other factors. Count stats (rebounds, strikeouts, receptions…) use a discrete distribution, and yardage and points use a normal curve sized for each stat.

Reading the results

  • Book (no-vig): the book's implied probability with the vig removed.
  • Model: the share of simulated games where your bet wins, not counting pushes.
  • Final: the model blended with the market using the "Trust market" slider. Books are hard to beat, so leaning partly on their number gives more realistic probabilities. Set it to 0% for the raw model.
  • EV/$100: the expected profit per $100 bet. BET means at least +$4, LEAN means +$1.50 to +$4, and PASS means everything else.
  • ¼ Kelly: a suggested bet size as a % of your bankroll, using a quarter of the Kelly formula for safety.

Where to get good inputs

  • NBA ratings & pace: basketball-reference.com, nba.com/stats (Advanced), teamrankings.com
  • NFL points for/against: pro-football-reference.com, teamrankings.com
  • MLB runs, starter FIP, bullpen ERA, park factors: fangraphs.com, baseball-reference.com
  • Injuries: check the official injury report right before the game. One missing star can be worth 3 to 6 points in the NBA.

Using the Real app

Open the game's market on realapp.com and type the away team's win % into Real app: away win %. Edge Lab treats it like a no-vig price (buying at 45% pays $122 profit per $100) and adds a Real market row so you can see whether Real or the sportsbook has the better price. Use the Live scores on Real button on each tab to follow the game.

Tips that actually help

  • Compare prices across books. A -105 instead of -115 on the same bet adds about 2% to your expected value.
  • Enter the injury adjustment. It's the biggest thing the season stats don't know about.
  • Be skeptical of big "edges." If the model shows +15 EV, a stat you entered is usually wrong or out of date.
